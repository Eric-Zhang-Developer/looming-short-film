## Story
I started with the two cheapest-looking items, s09 and s12, and both turned out to be different problems than the fix list suggested. s09 was not under-exposed so much as under-lit: the hemisphere light's sky colour is navy, so it contributed nearly nothing, and every pixel of the building was set by one spotlight. One cool directional moon fixed it in a single try (`s09_before.jpg` against `s09_after.jpg`). s12 looked like a framing slip until I did the arithmetic: the phone sits 4 mm off the desk, the blob's eyes 3 cm, a 2.85× throw puts the loom's top at 16.5 cm on the wall and the camera only saw to 11.6. I moved the camera, not the light.

The eye-level item was the one I trusted least, so I measured. I built a scratch page that poses the real rig and prints where the eyes joint sits. The hunch pose had his eyes 7 mm above the blob's, and s12's slump 6 mm below. One grid of lean and head angles gave me 0.0333 against 0.0324, and I checked it in a full-res crop (`eye_level_s11_85.9.jpg`). The custodian was the same story: the old sizes had been written to PIECES ratios the painter never followed, so the mop was being squeezed to a third of its drawn width and the arms were short. Reading the real aspect off the cut bitmaps and setting one long side per piece turned him from a smudge with a stick into a tortoise at a cart.

The cutaways were the one decision I'd defend if asked. The brief gave me two options for the six-step shrink, hold each step or cut closer. The beats can't move, so I cut closer, and the first render of the new Camera E (`s16_after.jpg`, frames 5–12) shows the blob going 1.5×, 1×, 0.75×, 0.58×, 0.42× against the same page corner. The wide never showed that. It costs the spec's "five cuts".
