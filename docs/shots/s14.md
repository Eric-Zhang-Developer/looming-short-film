## Light
- **Key: the fluorescent over the aisle (the Custodian's light).** `fluorescent({ flickerSeed: seedOf('s14', 1) })`: one dim `DirectionalLight` pointing down and tilted 15° toward camera, so the tiny blob's pool falls in front of it on the tray (README §3's smoke-paper finding). Intensity 0.6, **steady: no flicker in this shot.** Fluorescent tone (README §6). **Tiny blob shadow: a pool on the tray, scale 1.0× (≤ 1.1×).** The tiny blob sits under an overhead light, and its shadow is tiny. The `ceiling_header` flat keeps this light off the hero's desk, whose own fluorescent stays dead.
- **Rival (doc 03 rule 6, README §5): the hero's phone**, unchanged from s12/s13 (README §4): `phoneLight({ angle: 0.9 })` at (−0.010, 0.004, 0.114), aimed at the wall hit (0.061, 0.061, 0), intensity **0.6** (still dimmed). **Hero blob shadow: 7.1×** on the window wall behind the desk, center (0.061, 0.061). Face fill at 0.18. The phone's cone points toward −z, away from the aisle, so the cart and the Custodian never enter it.
- **The two shadows share the frame:** the hero's 7.1× shadow on the wall behind, and the Custodian's 1× pool on the cart in front. That contrast is the point of the shot (doc 03 rule 6: the shadow's direction shows which light is winning). The near-vertical fluorescent grazes the wall (Lambert ≈ 0 on a plane parallel to the light), so the wall stays dark and the hero's shadow keeps its contrast (the wall stays ≤ 0.3× the brightness of the aisle floor).
- **Ambient:** `ambient()` at 0.3× the default.
