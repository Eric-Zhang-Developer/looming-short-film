# 02 · Story

## Logline

A gator student has a final on Friday. The exam is a small drop of ink that swells a little every time he looks away from it, and its shadow fills the room, until one night in Marston Library he takes the first step.

## Theme

**The scary thing is always smaller than its shadow.** The turning point is not courage or rescue. It is one tiny, almost silly first step: uncapping a pen and writing your name. After that, the work turns out to be easier than the dread was.

Secondary note: **everyone has one.** The hero thinks he's alone with his shadow until he looks up.

## The visual rule

The whole film rests on one rule that the audience learns without words:

> **Light held low and close makes the shadow loom. Light from above makes it small.**

- The **phone** is a low, close light. It throws the Exam's shadow huge across walls and ceilings.
- **Light from above** drops the shadow into a small pool under the blob: the **desk lamp** at home, the **ceiling fluorescent** at Marston. Marston has no desk lamps. Its lights run on motion sensors, one zone per desk: stillness lets them time out, and movement brings them back.
- This is real optics: shadow scale ≈ (distance from light to wall) ÷ (distance from light to blob). See doc 04.

The rule must be shown **twice** before Marston (the opening phone beat and the home lamp beat), so the climax reads instantly.

### The meter

> **Every time he looks away, it grows. Every problem he solves, it shrinks.**

The blob's own size is the readable meter of dread (director, 2026-10-15). Each time he glances toward the exam (the blob, the calendar, the page, the laptop) and looks away to something easier, it gives a small nervous **gulp** on a beat and grows one step: from 1× at birth to 2.5× by Marston, where it sits at his desk eye to eye with him. Each solved problem shrinks it one step, down to pocket size. The shadow stays on top as how it *feels*: always bigger than the blob, always obeying the light rule. So the light shows what the dread feels like, and the size shows what the avoidance did.

The meter must be learned by the end of Act 2a: **five gulps, three of them by the lamp beat** (s03, s04, s06; then s07 and s11). Sizes, timings and the gulp's drawings are in `docs/shots/README.md` §1.

## Tone

A Pixar short in pacing and heart, a shadow play in look. Funny in the first act, quiet in the middle, warm at the end. The touchstone for the two held shots is the Sixth Station train sequence in *Spirited Away*: nobody hurries, and light moves across faces.

## Characters

**The Student.** A gator, freshman-to-sophomore age. A good kid, not lazy, just avoidant. Expressive through posture and a small set of eye shapes (doc 03). He never speaks. His arc is from hiding behind the phone's light to bringing the light back himself. At home he clicks the lamp off and chooses the dark. At Marston he goes still and the light dies on its own, and when he finally moves toward the work, it comes back.

**The Exam.** A drop of ink that rolls off the calendar and becomes a small round blob with two eyes. It is never evil. It grows because it is ignored, the way a neglected pet acts out: one visible step, a nervous gulp, each time he looks away, from the size of a coffee mug in its first scene to eye level with him at his desk. Up close in light from above it is small and a little nervous. It is the only thing in the film made of ink, not paper. It ends the film asleep in his hoodie pocket: not defeated, just manageable.

**The Roommates (2).** Oblivious. They walk straight through the shadow during game night. They exist to show how invisible this feeling is to everyone else.

**The Custodian.** An older gator mopping Marston at 3 a.m. His own blob is tiny and asleep on his cart. He never looks up and never helps. He is proof that it can be small, not a mentor. His passing wakes the light over our gator's desk for a moment without his noticing; our gator stays still, and it times out again. **He must not give the hero a light that stays on.** The hero does that.

**The Freshman.** First seen as one of the frozen gators in the Marston crowd reveal, then again in the final button. Planting them early makes the ending a payoff, not a new character.

## Beat sheet (3:00)

Timings are targets for the animatic. **Quiet** marks a held shot: locked camera, ambience only, light is the only thing that moves.

### Act 1: Birth (0:00–0:20)

- **0:00** Dawn over a paper Gainesville skyline. Push in to an apartment window.
- **0:06** The desk. A paper calendar. Friday is circled in red with a small drawn exam glyph (a sheet with a check mark). No legible words.
- **0:10** A tight insert on the calendar: a drop of ink beads on the red circle beside the exam glyph, runs down the sheet and perches on its curled bottom edge. It opens two eyes, looks up at the glyph it came from, then over at him, and lets go. Back on the desk it lands, swells to full size and wobbles toward him like a kitten.
- **0:15** He looks at it, shrugs and picks up his phone. **Rule shown, first time:** in the phone's low cyan glow, the blob's shadow climbs the wall, three times its size. He doesn't notice. Then, with its eyes on him, the blob gulps and grows a step, and its shadow swells with it. **The meter, first time.**

### Act 2a: Avoidance (0:20–1:00)

Four dodges that escalate. Each one makes the shadow bigger, each look-away makes the blob gulp and grow a step, and each dodge is funnier than the last. The day/night cycle speeds up through the window between them.

1. **The fridge (0:20).** He reorganizes the fridge instead of studying. The shadow stretches across the kitchen cabinets. He glances at the blob on its stool, looks straight back to a jar that's out of line, and it gulps.
2. **Game night (0:28).** Roommates on the couch with controllers. The shadow now spans the living room wall. A roommate walks straight through it to get a snack.
3. **The lamp (0:36). Rule shown, second time.** He sits at the desk and clicks the desk lamp on. The shadow snaps down to a small pool, and the blob blinks up at him, *right there*, plainly bigger than it was this morning. He flinches and clicks the lamp off. The shadow springs back. He takes the phone, and on the groove's next downbeat the blob gulps again. This is the moment the climax mirrors.
4. **The curtains (0:44).** The peak. He spends the night installing blackout curtains: drill, brackets, ladder. He glances at the blob on the floor and pulls the last panel shut. The finished room is pitch black except his phone, the blob gulps, and the shadow fills the ceiling.
5. **Scroll to sunrise (0:54).** In bed, face lit cyan, scrolling. Morning light creeps around the new curtains. Thursday.

### Act 2b: Marston (1:00–1:40)

- **1:00** Night exterior: Marston Library, a few lit windows. He walks in with his backpack.
- **1:06** The stacks. The shadow follows him in and fills the aisles. Fluorescent lights flicker as it passes.
- **1:12** He sits, opens his laptop, then opens his phone instead. The shadow closes over the table. He goes still over the phone, and the motion-sensor light over his desk times out. In the dark the blob gulps one last time: its peak, eye to eye with him.
- **1:18 · Quiet 1 (~10 s).** Head on the desk. The whole frame is shadow except the faint phone glow. Fluorescent hum. The shadow slowly breathes. Nothing happens. This is the darkest frame of the film.
- **1:28** He lifts his head and looks around. **Every gator in Marston has a shadow looming over them:** the one asleep on a textbook, the one with three energy drinks, the Freshman frozen in front of a blank page. Wide top-down shot: a grid of dark desks, each with its own shadow, a few faint phone glows.
- **1:35** The Custodian mops past. His blob is tiny, asleep on the cart. He doesn't look up. His passing wakes the light over our gator's desk: the shadow drops to a pool, and for a moment our gator sees his own blob as it is, big but nothing like its shadow. Our gator only watches the cart go by. As it leaves, his light times out again.

### Act 3: The first step (1:40–2:15)

- **1:40** His hand reaches for the pen and sets its point on the page. The same gesture as the home lamp beat, aimed at the work this time. The movement trips the sensor: a relay *clack*, the tube overhead stutters on, and the shadow drops to a small pool on the desk.
- **1:45** The blob sits in the light pool, eye to eye with him: bigger than it ever was at home, far smaller than its shadow, and nervous. One look between them. Hold it for about 2 seconds, then move on.
- **1:48** He turns the phone face-down. He uncaps a pen. He writes his name at the top of the page (an illegible squiggle). The shadow flinches.
- **1:54** Problem #1. He braces, and the blob braces too. He writes the answer in about three seconds, and the blob shrinks a step. He stares at the page in disbelief. *That was it?* This is the comedy beat and the thesis in one.
- **2:00** Problem #2, #3. A short time-lapse montage: pages turn, his light stays on because he keeps working, and with each problem the blob shrinks one step. By the third it's back to the size it was born, and it keeps going. He stands to stretch, and his movement wakes the dead light over a neighbor frozen at their phone. They look up at him and start working.

### Quiet 2: Dawn (2:15–2:40)

- **2:15 · Quiet 2 (~20 s).** Dawn. A locked wide shot of the Marston reading room. Gold light crawls through the tall windows across sleeping gators and empty desks. Our gator sits back. The blob, now hamster-sized, climbs into his hoodie pocket and falls asleep. No cuts and no music, only ambience and the light. This is the film's Sixth Station.

### Button (2:40–3:00)

- **2:40** Title card treatment, no words: a wipe on the paper to "next semester" (a new calendar sheet, a new circled date).
- **2:44** Marston at night. The Freshman sits frozen in a dead zone under an enormous shadow. Our gator walks past without stopping, and his movement wakes their light. He doesn't look back.
- **2:54** His pocket. The blob peeks out and yawns.
- **3:00** Black.

## Story rules

- **No legible text, anywhere.** Calendars, pages and screens use glyphs and scribbles only.
- **The hero takes the first step himself.** Nobody rescues him. Others can show it's possible; only he can do it.
- **The blob is never evil.** It's never attacked, chased off or destroyed.
- **It grows only by gulps and shrinks only by solved problems.** Each step snaps on a beat; it never slowly morphs. No text or thought bubble explains it, and the exam itself never gets a face. It still fits in his pocket at the end.
- **One idea per shot.** If a shot needs explaining, split it or cut it.

## Readability test

Before production locks, show the animatic to three viewers with no explanation. Afterwards, ask them only:
1. What was the ink blob?
2. Why did the shadow get big and small?
3. What changed for him?

If two of the three can't answer question 2, fix the rule demonstrations before animating anything else.
