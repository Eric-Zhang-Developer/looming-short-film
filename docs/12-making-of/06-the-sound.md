# 6 · The sound

## A score with no samples

When Eric first described the project at 7:11 he expected "AI music, AI-generated soundtrack music, everything." At 8:30, asked by the spec how the soundtrack should be made, he picked a different option: **"Synthesized in code."** [Doc 07](../07-process-log.md) records why. It keeps the whole film readable source, and it reuses the approach of his earlier code-built film *SUPER 1776*. [Doc 05](../05-sound.md) then set the rules:
- no samples, no vocals, no dialogue
- every sound is generated by a function
- every sound is placed by the name of a story beat, not a timestamp, so moving a beat moves its sound
- silence is an instrument, and both quiet shots get ambience only

Every line of the sound was written by agents, most of it between 9:37 and 12:02. Every audio done note in the repo says the same thing in one form or another: **they couldn't hear it.**

## The engine, built by ear-less agents

`snd-engine` was dispatched at 9:37 and merged at 9:47 (`f3d84db`). It delivered `src/audio/`, a plain Node synthesizer with no audio libraries. It has four voices (felt piano, music box, warm pad, sub swell), envelopes and filters, a 32-bit WAV writer, and a mix tool that renders all 180 seconds in **16.0 s** on the laptop and writes byte-identical files every run ([`done/snd-engine.md`](../../done/snd-engine.md)).

With no ears, the builder checked its work by eye. It rendered spectrograms and waveforms and read them (`evidence/snd-engine/lampclick_spec.png`, `piano_c4_spec.png`). That's how it found its first two bugs:
- The lamp's filament "tink" was a 0.4 s pure tone that ended in a splash.
- The piano had a full-band vertical line at 1.8 s, where the damper hit a hard edge.

Its honest read of its own instrument:

> "The piano C4 is dark and soft … It probably reads as a mellow synth or electric piano rather than a real felt piano, and the Schroeder tail may sound slightly metallic."

The spec didn't say what the Exam's motif should be, so the builder chose and recorded it. It's four music-box notes, C5 E5 A5 D#5, each a few cents out of tune (+14, −10, +18, −12) and 0.55 s apart. That small, slightly sour figure is the monster's voice.

## The fear motif, faced, turns into the theme

`snd-score` (merged 10:01, `cc65159`) wrote the film's music as a flat list of note events, each hung on a beat name ([`done/snd-score.md`](../../done/snd-score.md)). Its key idea is in one paragraph of the done note:
- The theme, on felt piano, is **C E A G E F D**.
- It takes the Exam motif's opening leaps (up a major third, up a fourth) and plays them *in tune*.
- The motif's sour fall to D# becomes a step down to G.

In the builder's words: "The fear motif, faced, turns into the theme." The theme first enters at the moment he writes his name, one held C, then one note per solved problem.

Around that it built the rest of the score:
- a 120 bpm avoidance groove that adds one layer per dodge, with a sub swell rising from 44 to 56 Hz under the growing shadow
- a stop-time that leaves the home lamp clicks in the open
- four sparse chords for the Marston reveal, one per shadow
- nothing at all in either quiet shot (`evidence/snd-score/timeline.png` shows the gaps)
- a montage that ends "open on Gsus4", with the resolution to C held back until the button, after the Freshman's light

## The identical click

Doc 05 made one sound the most important in the film: the lamp click. It's "designed once, carefully", and it has to be the *identical* buffer every time it's heard, so the ear links the home lamp to the turn. Keeping it identical took four tasks.

1. **snd-engine** proved the three clicks were sample-identical in the foley stem.
2. **snd-score** found that the final mix broke it. The loudness normalizer ran in "dynamic" mode, its gain shifted with the surrounding music, and the clicks in `mix.wav` differed by up to 2,078 LSB. The builder didn't own the mixer, so it reported the problem instead of patching it.
3. **contract-audio** (`8bf81df`, 10:13) added a limiter on the music stem so the normalizer could run in **linear** mode: one gain for the whole film. It proved that mode by showing `mix.wav = g·premix` to within 0.5 LSB over all 8,640,000 samples. The click test still **failed**: home against Marston, 8 LSB ([`done/contract-audio.md`](../../done/contract-audio.md)). The builder tracked down the cause. It was 7.66 LSB of piano reverb tail from the groove's last stop-time chord at 35.5 s, still ringing at 36.5 s under the home click. The mixer was fine, and the score was leaking under the click.
4. **snd-score-clicks** (`ae98d1b`, 10:15) gated the music stem to true zero for each click's 0.3 s, with a 40 ms fade out and a 20 ms fade in. The result: `max diff … [0,0,0] LSB` ([`done/snd-score-clicks.md`](../../done/snd-score-clicks.md)).

Four agents, from 9:47 to 10:15, for a click that is bit-for-bit the same sound wherever it plays.

The rule outlived the lamp. When Marston lost its desk lamps ([chapter 7](07-marston-has-no-lamps.md)), the click count changed: home on and home off are one identical pair, and a new `sensorWake` (a relay clack, three strike ticks and a fluorescent ping) is a second identical pair for s15 and s18. Doc 05 asked for the wake's transient to be built from the click's, pitched down, "so the ear links the two". `contract-sensor` re-proved both pairs at 0 LSB in the final mix ([`done/contract-sensor.md`](../../done/contract-sensor.md)).

## Thirty sounds nobody has heard

`snd-foley` (9:53) synthesized the eleven sounds doc 05 listed and three ambience beds the spec named but nobody had written ([`done/snd-foley.md`](../../done/snd-foley.md)). `snd-foley-2` (12:02) added eleven more that the shot specs asked for: a thumb flick, jar clinks, a carton slosh, TV bloops, roommate footsteps, a win jingle, ladder creaks, a curtain rod snap and others ([`done/snd-foley-2.md`](../../done/snd-foley-2.md)). Between them, `snd-cues` placed 78 temp cues for the animatic. It refused to fake the missing ones: "None is faked from another sound."

Each foley builder ended with a guess, sound by sound, at what the thing would actually sound like, and they're the most candid lines in the repo. A few:
- **penScratch:** "may sound more like sandpaper hiss than a pen, because there's no pitch to the nib."
- **inkRoll:** "might read as a draining sink rather than a rolling drop."
- **mopOnTile:** "may read as brushing or sweeping more than mopping."
- **dawnBirds:** "Will read as 'synth birds', not a specific species."

They checked what they could check. Every sound renders identically twice, stays finite, has peaks under 0 dBFS and starts and ends at silence (`evidence/snd-foley/check.txt`). They read every spectrogram and fixed what looked wrong:
- a drill hiss that covered the whole spectrum
- a mop bright to 16 kHz
- buffers that stopped on a step, which shows up as a vertical line

Twice a builder hit a test it didn't own that conflicted with its brief, and both times it reported the conflict instead of weakening the test.

## What's still open

By noon the sound for the whole 3:00 existed, ahead of the picture. Doc 07 notes "Sound is ahead of schedule" at the wave 2 merge. What hasn't happened is listening. Whether anyone has played the mix on laptop speakers and earbuds, as doc 05 asks, isn't recorded anywhere. The Act 1 preview carried temp sound at −16.3 LUFS with peaks of −1.7 dBFS. The orchestrator noted the peaks need to come down to −4 in the sound pass.

`NOW.md` carries the rest:
- **Mix headroom:** the premix sits at 12.36 dB peak-to-loudness against the normalizer's 12.5 dB limit, so some foley is capped under spec level.
- **Keep the score synthesized, or switch to licensed tracks?** That's waiting on the director.
- **The gulp, specced but not built.** Each time the blob grows it will play the motif's first two notes, one semitone lower than the last, so the ear hears the same creature getting heavier. Each solved problem will answer with the same two notes falling, one semitone higher each time ([doc 05](../05-sound.md), "Leitmotifs").
