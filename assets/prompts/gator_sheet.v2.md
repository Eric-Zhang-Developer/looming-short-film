## 3. Asset

A revision of a character sheet for "the Student," the film's hero: a young alligator college student made as a cut-paper puppet. Two images are attached. The first is the style tile (the look of the whole film). The second is the approved previous version of this exact sheet. Repaint the second image: the same fourteen pieces, in the same three-row layout, at the same shared scale, with the same colours and proportions. Keep the full side view (top left) and every rig part exactly as they are there, shape for shape. Do not redesign him. Change only the things listed below.

The design, unchanged: flat simple geometric shapes like a mid-century picture-book illustration, a strong silhouette, a tall rounded head running into a long straight blunt snout held level, a semicircle eye bump on top of the head, green upper jaw, cream lower jaw, two to four tiny cream triangle tooth tips along a gently upturned smile line, a boxy oversized cream hoodie with the hood down as a soft rolled collar, round green mitten hands, two short stubby green legs (about one fifth of his standing height) with small feet pointing forward, and a long tapering tail with a cream underside and a single row of small darker-green rounded scallops along its top.

What changes:

(A) The front view (top row, second piece) must read instantly as an alligator, not a frog or a bear. He faces the viewer and his long snout points straight at us, so it is foreshortened: a broad, rounded, slightly squarish green muzzle that sits low on the front of the face and covers the lower half of the head, wider than it is tall, overlapping the head like a separate cut layer. On top of the muzzle, near its front edge, two small raised rounded nostril bumps side by side, each with a tiny dark nostril dot. Along the bottom edge of the muzzle, where the upper jaw meets the lower jaw, a row of four to six tiny cream triangle tooth tips peeks down and up, small and friendly. Under the muzzle only a thin cream sliver of lower jaw shows. The eye bumps stay as two semicircles on top of the head. Remove the large cream patch on the face and throat completely: there is no cream mask, bib or throat shape; the neck above the hoodie collar is green. No eyes painted (they are a separate sheet). Still painted without arms, as before.

(B) The three-quarter head (top row, fourth piece) must be a real three-quarter view: the head turned about 45 degrees toward the viewer's right. We see the front and the right side of the head at once. The snout angles away from us toward the right and back into depth, so it looks shorter than in the side view and its far side is hidden. Both eye bumps are visible: the near one fully, the far one partly, peeking over the top of the head behind it. Paint both eyes in them as in the side view (cream circle, large black pupil looking right, tiny cream dot in the pupil), the far eye partly hidden by the head. The two nostril bumps show near the snout tip, the near one larger. The jaw seam runs back along the near side with its tooth tips. No cream throat patch. Head, snout and jaws are one piece, without the body.

(C) Torn paper edges. Every one of the fourteen pieces gets a visible pale torn fibre rim along its whole outline, 2 to 6 pixels wide, exactly like the torn edges of the navy wall and the brown desk in the style tile: a thin irregular band of paler, whiter exposed paper core where the card was torn, slightly uneven in width, hugging the outline. The rim is a little wider and raggeder on the tissue-weight cream hoodie than on the green card. No felt, no fuzz, no pile, no hairs or fibres sticking out past the edge.

(D) The two legs (bottom row) are cut flat across the top: the hip end is a clean straight cut edge in the same flat green, with the torn rim. Do not draw the leg tops as lighter ovals or cylinder ends. Nothing is drawn as a 3D object with a top and a side.

(E) Spacing. Keep every piece at least 60 px from every other piece and from all four edges of the image, measured between the pieces' bounding rectangles, so no two pieces touch, overlap or share a bounding box and nothing touches the frame. If needed, make all pieces slightly smaller together, at one shared scale, rather than crowding them.

Every piece is one complete closed shape. Parts include the small extra overlap at their joints, finished as clean ends.

Palette: gator green #6F8F5E, a slightly darker green only for the tail scallops, cream #F2E8D5 for the hoodie, pocket, lower jaw, tooth tips and torn rims, black only for pupils and nostril dots. Nothing in the artwork is magenta, pink or purple. No navy, no amber, no kraft.

Banned: no text, letters, numbers or labels next to the pieces. No eyebrow, no brow ridge, no scales, no spikes, no claws, no tongue, no gums. No cast shadows, no drop shadows, no outline glow, no piece lighter on one side than the other.

Size: the largest landscape size available.
